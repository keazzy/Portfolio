Impulse is an AI-native, agentic CRM built to replace the fragmented stack of 5+ tools that GTM teams juggle: prospecting, outreach, pipeline management, automation, and more. I joined as the founding designer to define the product's design direction and build the initial design system from the ground up.

I focused on the company's most critical challenge: proving that an all-in-one CRM could be simpler, not more complex. Legacy CRMs like Salesforce and HubSpot are infamous for clunky interfaces and endless manual work. My work established a new paradigm: a modern, automation-first workspace where the system does the heavy lifting while the user stays in control.

Reimagining the CRM paradigm

For two decades, CRMs have been databases dressed up as productivity tools. Sales teams spend hours logging data, updating fields, and hunting for context. The result? What Impulse's founders call "the quiet burnout behind GTM teams everywhere."

Impulse promised something radically different: a CRM that works for you, not the other way around. One that assigns tasks, updates pipelines, researches leads, and drafts personalized outreach while you sleep. But designing for that vision meant confronting a fundamental challenge: how do you make proactive automation feel natural when users are conditioned to reactive tools?

Through early user research with GTM teams, the tension became clear: people wanted automation but didn't trust black-box AI. They wanted consolidation but feared another bloated interface. I established three core design principles:

• Show, don't hide: I designed surfaces that showed what Impulse did and why, building trust through transparency.

• Space, not clutter: I redesigned the dashboard to prioritize breathing room, surfacing only what's actionable right now.

• Context over chrome: I unified prospecting, pipeline, and automation into a single contextual workspace that adapts to what you're doing.

The hardest part was reimagining what a CRM should feel like. Users are conditioned to HubSpot's busy, form-heavy interface. Would simpler feel less capable? The validation came early: we onboarded 5 companies before launch based on designs alone. Teams saw the mocks and immediately understood the vision.

A design system built for speed

I built the initial design system around a single principle: what ships matters more than perfect design artifacts. I focused on high-leverage components (AI agent cards, task surfaces, pipeline views, automation states) that would cover 80% of the product.

The visual language reinforced Impulse's positioning: clean, modern, human. I created patterns for showing "Impulse did this for you" moments: subtle indicators that surfaced AI work without overwhelming the user.

The result: we shipped the full V1 product and began scaling to paying customers around the world.

Bringing the vision to life

Impulse is now live with paying customers globally, delivering on the promise to consolidate GTM workflows and buy back time for teams. The design direction I established continues to evolve, but the foundation remains: a modern CRM that works at the speed of you, not against you.
