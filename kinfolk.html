While working as a Senior Product Designer at Kinfolk VC, backing Africa's next generation of technology entrepreneurs, I led the design of Cuuro: an AI-driven due diligence platform that transformed how our firm managed investments. What started as an internal tool to tame the chaos of deal flow, scattered due diligence docs, and manual portfolio tracking caught the attention of other VCs who wanted to know what we were using.

Bringing order to venture chaos

Venture capital runs on scattered information. Deal flow arrives through every channel imaginable. Due diligence lives across email threads, Google Docs, and endless spreadsheets. Portfolio updates get lost in overflowing inboxes. At Kinfolk, we needed a better way to manage investments from first contact to exit.

The challenge intensified when we decided to build Cuuro for the market: how do you serve both investors conducting diligence and founders raising capital without compromising either side?

I designed a dual-sided platform anchored by three core experiences:

For investors: Deal flow as a command center

I redesigned deal tracking around a central dashboard that surfaces everything about a startup in one view: email trails, notes, files, tasks, and a collaborative risk scorecard. Investors could track portfolio companies across stages (awareness, initial contact, work in progress, term sheet) and invite specialists like financial analysts to add risk reviews before deploying capital.

Another breakthrough was AI Meeting Recording: We designed a system that captured investor-founder conversations, automatically generated transcripts, extracted action items, and surfaced AI-generated follow-up questions. Investors could reference specific moments in conversations, cite sources directly from meeting transcripts, and build institutional knowledge from every interaction. This transformed scattered meeting notes into structured, searchable intelligence.

For founders: Transparency as a competitive advantage

Founders got a streamlined application platform with real-time status tracking and a centralized data room that made sharing documents with investors instant. But the feature I enjoyed designing most was financial tracking: founders could connect their bank accounts and accounting systems, and AI would categorize transactions automatically. They could ask the AI to categorize expenses or set custom rules, creating transparency for their team and investors alike.

This reduced the time from application submission to first investment, removing friction from the fundraising process.

AI as the Bridge

AI-powered memo generation, financial analysis, and transaction categorization served both sides by creating structure where there was chaos. I designed every AI feature with transparency: showing source documents, surfacing calculations, and giving users control over automated decisions.

From internal tool to market product

Cuuro launched internally at Kinfolk VC, and the impact was immediate: the team saved 20+ hours per week and accelerated diligence cycles by 120%. More importantly, the dual-sided design changed how we engaged with founders. One portfolio founder, Chidozie Okonkwo, said:

"They do not feel like investors, they feel like partners, partners who have managed to use tech to define how venture capital will engage with founders in our collective quest to build the future. Of all the investor relationships we have, the one we share with Kinfolk is the richest."

As other VCs expressed interest, we began preparing Cuuro for external testing. When I transitioned off the project, I left behind a complete V1 product ready for broader market validation. Cuuro proved that the right technology can transform venture capital from a transactional process into a genuine partnership.
